Hands-on evaluation

Follow a question back to its source.

Try three complete journeys: a patient clarifies an incomplete discharge bundle, a ward checks documents before sharing them, and a patient brings a scanned letter. AI helps at the relevant steps in each journey.

Use the supplied invented examples. Demo roles can be switched and do not verify real identity. No external messages are sent. Public uploads accept only the example PDFs below.

Documents has direct links to document checks, medical explanations and Is the answer already here? You can also check a question from My questions > New question. Open a question itself to read its reply and continue the conversation.

On a phone, use the bottom navigation; Docs opens Documents. Open Options at the top to switch between patient and ward views. On desktop, use View ward or View patient in the header.

Use Add another question for a separate conversation. The ward starts with open questions; Resolved retrieves previous conversations and Action required lists those needing a reply. Staff can choose Send reply directly; saving a draft is optional.

Case 1 - Patient and ward

The letter mentions a rehabilitation sheet. Where is it?

Giorgio has his discharge letter, but the rehabilitation programme and appointment reminder are still in the ward's case file. Help him distinguish an unfamiliar expression from information he actually lacks, then resolve the gap with the ward.

  1. Read what the patient received. Open the demo and choose Start as a patient. In Documents, open the discharge letter. Notice the references to a rehabilitation sheet and appointment reminder. Choose Back to documents.
  2. Look for an existing answer. Select Is the answer already here? Enter What does follow-up mean?, choose Add another question, then enter What date and time is my follow-up appointment? Choose Check my documents first.
  3. Compare the questions with their sources. The letter defines follow-up but supplies no appointment date or time. Inspect the AI-selected passages. Leave the first question unselected if its passage answers your concern. Nothing has been sent to the ward. Use Documents in the navigation to inspect the wider document gap before sending a request.
  4. Find what needs clarification. In Documents, select Check for missing information, then Check selected documents. Wait for the check to finish. Review each suggested question beside its exact source. The letter refers to supporting documents the patient cannot yet see; model outputs may vary.
  5. Send only the questions that matter. Select the rehabilitation-sheet question and any other unanswered point you want to raise, then choose Send selected questions together. If the sheet is not suggested, ask where it is through My questions > New question. Open a question from My questions to see its conversation and source. If you already have a referenced sheet on paper, add it before asking for another copy.
  6. Open the ward's reply screen. Choose View ward, then open the rehabilitation question. The patient's question and your reply box appear in the same conversation.
  7. Ask for a reply suggestion. Choose Suggest a reply from the documents. Wait for the suggestion below the editor. The rehabilitation sheet is already in the ward case file: inspect whether the suggestion recommends sharing it. If the service is busy, you can retry when available or write your reply directly.
  8. Review and edit. Read the suggestion and open its cited passages. Choose Use in my reply editor if it helps, then edit the reply as needed. An existing draft is replaced only after you confirm.
  9. Share the rehabilitation sheet. If the suggestion identifies it, choose Review and share document, check the recipient and selected file, then confirm. Otherwise open Share a document with the patient, select Rehabilitation programme and choose Share selected documents. Your reply text stays in the editor. After sharing, update it to tell the patient that the sheet is now in Documents.
  10. Add a new PDF only if needed. This example already includes the required sheet. To try adding another file, choose Upload a PDF for this reply. Use a supplied example, review its pages, then choose Accept text and return to reply. Share that file explicitly if the patient needs it.
  11. Send the reply. Choose Send reply. Sharing a PDF and sending a reply are separate choices; you can answer without an attachment or an AI suggestion.
  12. Read it as the patient. Choose View patient and open the question directly. Read the reply, then open Documents to see the shared rehabilitation PDF.
  13. Continue the conversation. Return to the question and choose Still unclear: ask for more details. Write a follow-up and choose Ask in this conversation. Switch to the ward view and answer it in the same thread.
  14. Resolve it as the patient. Switch back to the patient view, open the latest reply and choose I understand, question resolved. The ward can now find it under Resolved.

What to verify

AI helps separate an answer already in the letter from a missing document. Patient checks cannot use ward-only files. Only selected questions are sent; AI reply drafts require staff review. Follow-ups stay in one thread, and the patient decides when it is resolved.

Optional: send a reminder from a waiting question before the ward replies. Only that question should be highlighted for staff.

Case 2 - Ward initiates

Check the discharge bundle before the patient needs to ask.

The ward already holds the letter, rehabilitation programme and appointment reminder. Check them together, share them, then see whether the patient can find an answer without opening a new ward conversation.

  1. Start before delivery. Return to the start page using the AfterWard logo and choose Start as ward staff. Open Documents. All three PDFs are in the case file and have not yet been shared.
  2. Check the complete bundle. Select Check before sharing, leave all three documents selected, then choose Check selected documents. AfterWard checks the documents available to the ward for missing details and conflicting instructions.
  3. Review the findings in context. Open the source for any suggested clarification. The programme describes the exercises and their start condition; the reminder gives the follow-up appointment. They address references that were incomplete in the letter alone. Review any remaining issue before delivery. An empty result means only that this run found no supported clarification points.
  4. Deliver once. Choose Back to documents, tick Include when sharing on all three files, choose Share selected documents, review the recipient and confirm. Switch to View patient and open Documents. The PDFs are available without the patient uploading them again.
  5. Find an answer in the delivered bundle. From Documents, choose Is the answer already here? Ask What date and time is my follow-up appointment? and choose Check my documents first. Inspect the cited reminder: it states 15 December at 09:30. If that answers the question, leave it unselected and choose I have what I need.
  6. Check the outcome. Open My questions. Reading an answer from the documents has not created a ward task. If the source was insufficient, the patient could instead select the question and send it; staff would receive the linked conversation.

What to verify

The ward can check existing documents before delivery. The patient receives the same bundle and can retrieve an answer from it. This demonstrates how AI may prevent a clarification request; it does not measure hospital workload savings. Changed documents require a fresh check.

Case 3 - Patient brings a PDF

Turn a scanned letter into wording the patient can understand.

This three-page example contains a discharge letter, attendance certificate and recovery certificate. It follows the structure of a real bundle, rebuilt with invented identities and events; it contains no original patient images, signatures or hidden source text.

Download the three-page scanned exampleSimple discharge letterRehabilitation programmeAppointment reminder
  1. Bring the scan. Download the three-page scanned example. Return to the start page and choose Start with a PDF. In the empty case, choose Upload a PDF, select the unchanged file, then Read the PDF.
  2. Let AfterWard read it. AfterWard reads the pages and shows its progress. When reading finishes, the original pages and editable transcriptions appear for review. Reading may take several minutes.
  3. Verify the transcription. Compare every page with its original. Check Jordan Hart, the dates, 500 mg, 2 days, 11:15, and heading-to-text associations. Blank declaration fields should stay blank. Correct any mistakes in the extracted text, tick the review checkbox and choose Accept reviewed text.
  4. Understand the clinical wording. In the opened document, choose Explain medical wording. Open a term under Medical wording, explained and read its everyday explanation beside the original passage. AfterWard prepares these explanations while reading the document. If it is still working, a spinner shows that the explanations are being prepared. Once ready, they open straight away.
  5. Ask your own question. Under Ask about this document, enter What does mg mean here? and copy Paracetamol 500 mg into Passage from your document. Choose Ask AfterWard. Check that the answer explains the unit instead of repeating the quotation. The question and answer stay beside their source. Questions needing missing clinical information can be prepared for the ward; unrelated requests are declined.
  6. Check whether instructions need clarification. Return through the source document link and choose Check documents, then Check selected documents. Inspect any findings against the reviewed text and original PDF. The explanation of a term and a check for missing information serve different needs: understanding a diagnosis does not establish that the discharge instructions are complete.
  7. Prepare a focused question if needed. Select a relevant finding and send it explicitly. Alternatively, return to a term and choose Still unclear? Ask the ward; this prepares an editable question. Choose Check my documents first to look for an existing answer before deciding whether to send it. Reading an explanation alone never creates a staff task.

What to verify

AI reads the scan, explains clinical wording and checks the reviewed document within one patient journey. Compare each explanation and suggested question with the original document. The patient chooses whether anything still needs the ward.

This example demonstrates patient-led intake. Trying a ward reply in the demo does not connect a hospital that has not adopted AfterWard. If reading or checking cannot finish, AfterWard tells you what to do next. The original PDF stays available.

Try it together on two devices

In an existing case, select Connect another device. Open the exact one-use link on the second device within ten minutes, without starting another case. Both screens must show the same case code. Questions, replies and AI progress update when you are not editing a field.

Scope of this demonstration

You can read scanned PDFs, check documents, understand medical wording, ask linked questions, send reminders, reply to several questions and work together on two devices.

AI suggestions can be wrong. Read the original instructions and ask the ward when something is unclear. This demo does not verify patient or staff identity and is not connected to a hospital.
