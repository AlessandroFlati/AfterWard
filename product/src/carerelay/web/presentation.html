AfterWard - Health and Wellbeing

Clearer discharge documents. Fewer unresolved questions.

AfterWard checks discharge documents, explains medical wording and finds existing answers before patients contact the ward. Google Gemma on AMD links suggestions to sources, with human review and connected conversations.

The product

A discharge letter can contain correct medical information while leaving a person unsure what to do next. A referenced sheet may be missing from the patient's packet, an appointment may lack an arrangement, or a familiar clinical term may be unfamiliar at home. Each unresolved question can also create work for the ward.

AfterWard connects document understanding with the conversation that follows. A ward can check the documents it already holds before sharing a discharge bundle. A patient can open that bundle without uploading it again, or start with a PDF when their hospital has not adopted the service. The AI locates possible missing or conflicting instructions and shows the exact source passages. Gemma selects unfamiliar clinical wording and prepares everyday explanations in the background after OCR. A shared server cache makes prepared explanations immediately available beside their original passages, without another model call. Patients can also ask in their own words alongside an exact extract, with the reviewed document supplied as context. If the document does not provide enough context, the patient can prepare a clarification question for the ward. Before sending a new question, the patient can search their available documents and forward only the questions that still need the ward.

Staff can answer directly, request an optional AI draft, or share a document already in the case file. When an attachment is requested and the ward already holds it, the AI can recommend that document for explicit sharing instead of asking staff to look for it again. Follow-ups remain in the original conversation. The patient decides when the question is resolved.

Google Gemma-4-31B-IT runs through the AMD Model API for image transcription and text tasks. The server validates structured responses and source references, preserves document revisions and makes failed requests explicit. The hosted English demo provides guided cases and packaged example PDFs, including a reconstructed three-page scan.

Our evaluation uses frozen synthetic document cases, with errors retained. It is not clinical validation. The next step is a controlled ward pilot measuring net staff time and patient understanding, supported by verified identities, an appropriate healthcare data-processing agreement and independent clinical review. Selected FHIR, schema and audit foundations come from the author's MIT-licensed TrustedRisk project and are disclosed in the repository.

What AMD and Google contribute

The browser calls an English Python application hosted on Railway. SQLite stores each case, document revision and conversation on a persistent volume. PDFium renders scanned pages. The server sends images or bounded source text to Gemma-4-31B-IT through the fixed AMD Model API endpoint. OCR uses reasoning none. Documentary review uses medium reasoning for factual extraction, findings and a conditional conflict comparison. Patient question checking and optional ward drafts use medium. Ward output can also identify cited, undelivered documents to share. The server validates those identifiers and sources; staff review and confirm the recipient and files. Prepared clinical explanations use medium reasoning and appear directly as AI-generated beside the original passage. The Ask about this document action uses none reasoning for a free-form question, an exact unique source extract and the complete reviewed document. It declines unrelated requests and withholds answers that need missing clinical information. It rejects unsupported numbers and simple echoes, without claiming semantic verification. Staff review is optional. If a response flags insufficient context, the explanation is withheld and the patient can prepare a clarification question. Exact source references, response validation and revision checks constrain the workflow, but do not prove clinical correctness. The product invokes real inference and never substitutes recorded output for a failed call. Clinical explanations are prepared in the background with medium reasoning and stored in a shared persistent cache keyed by exact document text, model and prompt version. Corrected text invalidates reuse; identical documents in other sessions reuse the result.

The distinction between what the ward possesses and what the patient has received drives the analysis. A sheet in the ward archive does not answer the patient's question until it is shared. The same workflow can prevent clarification needs before discharge, locate existing answers afterwards and preserve the remaining human conversation until the patient understands.

Evaluation scope

The frozen English research sample contains 100 patient document cases and 100 ward cases. Patient v7 medium recovered 68 expected findings, raised 38 additional findings and missed 4: precision 64.15%, recall 94.44%, exact cases 73%. Ward v2 medium recorded precision 98.31%, recall 96.67% and exact cases 97%.

A repeated patient v6 run scored 57% exact, 65.38% precision and 70.83% recall, with 21 API/output failures, including 15 provider outages. Both runs also encountered one unexpected acquisition block; errors remain in the results. The provider disruption prevents a clean conclusion about prompt quality. The live app retains its v7 adaptation.

These are different tasks on clustered synthetic examples, including acquisition/workflow gates. The tests are not clinical validation, an OCR benchmark or an evaluation of every integrated web feature.

Business value to test

The intended first buyer is a hospital discharge or continuity-of-care service. The measurable hypothesis is less net staff time spent reconstructing context and handling avoidable clarification contacts. A pilot must subtract review time, false alerts and new work from any time saved. Low inference cost makes repeated document checks feasible, but staffing, integration and governance dominate the business case. No reduction in readmissions, clinical errors or hospital cash expenditure has been measured.

Illustrative scenario: 10,000 cases per year, two net staff minutes saved per case and EUR30/hour capacity value. Against EUR5,000 fixed plus EUR1,200 variable annual budgets, the resulting capacity value exceeds the assumed budget by EUR3,800. Break-even is 1.24 net minutes per case. These assumptions require a pilot; released capacity is not guaranteed cash saving.

The low token envelope shown in the pitch uses a dated commercial reference. AMD development quota coefficients have no monetary value and are not a production tariff.

The ward replies in context

Back in the patient case, staff open the question in its conversation. They can reply directly or ask for an AI suggestion. The rehabilitation sheet is already held by the ward, so the assistant can recommend sharing it. Staff inspect the document and confirm delivery, then send their reply. Extra evidence is optional. If the patient still needs detail, the follow-up stays in this thread. The patient confirms when the question is resolved, and the ward can focus on the conversations still open.

Recording direction

What remains a human decision

AfterWard explains wording and highlights questions. It does not diagnose or choose treatment. Source and revision checks reject invalid output, but cannot guarantee medical correctness. Staff review drafts before sending. The public demo accepts only the supplied example PDFs. Verified identities and appropriate health-data agreements are requirements for a hospital pilot.

Recording direction
